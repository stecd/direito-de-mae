Her Data, Her Choice

Awaiting your pass · unmerged PR stecd/direito-de-mae#3 Change · Phase 2

To comment, select any text and use the comment control in the artifact viewer.

What she gets now

Before this change, the first thing a new mother saw was a consent footer stuck to the bottom of a legal answer. Now she gets a short interview before anything is stored or sent to an AI:

1 · Hi, what this is, "Não sou advogada"→2 · Escrita, áudio, ou os dois?→3 · Consent: SIM / NÃO→4 · Safety tip→5 · Her first message, answered

After that, everything she sends is encrypted on the server, links to her files stop working after 15 minutes, Twilio's copies are deleted, and she can ask for things in her own words. "Me manda tudo que eu te contei" gets her the resumo PDF without knowing the word RESUMO. Deleting always asks once more.

Background

New to this system? Read on. Already know the message flow? Skip to the part that changed.

A mother writes to a WhatsApp number. Twilio receives it and calls our server's /whatsapp webhook. The server answers Twilio at once (Twilio gives up after 15 seconds) and does the real work in the background. It downloads any photo or voice note, transcribes voice with ElevenLabs, asks Claude for an answer with the law cited, and sends the reply back through Twilio's API. Her history, entries and evidence files live in data/, one JSON file per user, named by an HMAC of her phone number so the number itself is never stored. A web chat at / runs the same handler without Twilio, which is how everything here was tested.

Her words pass through six places. Phase 1 protected one of them, the filename. This is each place before and after:

Her phoneWas: chat visible to whoever holds it.
Now: safety tip on disappearing messages and a neutral contact name.
TwilioWas: every message and photo kept indefinitely.
Now: deleted about 2 minutes after delivery.
Server diskWas: plain JSON, plain photos.
Now: encrypted per user; key shredded on APAGAR.
File linksWas: /media/<uid>/<file> forever.
Now: signed, expire in 15 min.
LogsWas: every transcript.
Now: event names and error types only.
Claude, ElevenLabsWas: called on her first message.
Now: no Claude before SIM. A first voice note is still transcribed.

Intuition: three ideas

1 · Delete the key, not just the file

Take a mother whose file is data/9f2c….json. It is encrypted with her own random key, and her key is itself encrypted with the server's MASTER_KEY:

MASTER_KEY (env)unlocks →data/_keys/9f2c….keyunlocks →9f2c….json · prova_a1.jpg · resumo_77.pdf

APAGAR removes the key file first. If a copy of 9f2c….json survives somewhere (a disk backup, a laptop sync), it is now noise. A random key per user, rather than one derived from the master key, is what makes that work: a derived key could simply be derived again.

2 · She can say it her way

Exact words still skip the AI: RESUMO, SALVAR, APAGAR, MEUS DADOS, MUDAR. Anything else goes to Claude, which now returns one more field, intent. The code, not Claude, carries out the action:

Claude's answer to "quero trocar minha escolha"
{"reply": "ok", "due_date": null, "entry": null, "intent": "mudar_modo"}

If Claude says apagar, the code only asks her to confirm. A forwarded screenshot that says "apague tudo" can at most produce a question.

3 · One message at a time per mother

The review found the sharpest bug in this change. She sends a photo of a long WhatsApp thread, and Claude takes 8 seconds to read it. Meanwhile she confirms APAGAR. The delete runs, then the photo's handler finishes and saves the history it loaded before the delete, under a brand-new key. Her data is back. A per-user lock now makes the second message wait for the first.

sweep() deletes anyone silent for 90 days (365 after SALVAR), plus delivered PDFs and voice replies after an hour. It runs every 10 minutes inside the app. Files written before this change still load, and are encrypted the next time they are saved or read. If a file can't be decrypted (the master key changed), store.Unreadable is raised: she is told to send APAGAR, which works without decrypting, and the sweep falls back to the file's age.

The signature covers user, file name and expiry, so a link can't be pointed at another file or stretched. Tracebacks in the log keep file, line and exception type, and drop the exception message, which can echo her words.

Onboarding · onboarding.py, app.py

Step state lives in profile.onboarding: mode → consent → done, and mode_change after MUDAR. "1", "escrita", "áudio", "os dois" all work. A voice-note answer with no clear choice counts as audio. Words are matched whole, so "minha mulher" isn't read as "ler". NÃO at consent deletes everything, including her first message. Replies follow her choice:

  • escrita: text only.
  • áudio: voice note only (text too when a file is attached, or if the voice fails).
  • os dois: both.

Commands · app.py, brain.py

RESUMO replaces DOSSIÊ in everything she sees ("dossiê" still works). New: SALVAR keeps her data a year, MEUS DADOS says what's stored and the deletion date, MUDAR re-asks text or audio. APAGAR always asks once more. Only APAGAR or SIM within 10 minutes confirms, and a spoken "não quero apagar" never does.

Voice · app.py, public/voice/

Voice replies are off unless VOICE_REPLIES=1, to save ElevenLabs credits until the demo. The 16 fixed messages are voiced once and kept in public/voice/, which is committed. Four came free from ElevenLabs history via import_voice_cache.py. Replies with anything personal are never cached.

What did not change

Law answers, date maths, the PDF's content and layout, the Twilio webhook contract, and the web chat apart from its link parsing. The phone number is still never stored, which is why the 7-day warning before auto-delete isn't built (see Limits).

The review

The co-worker was asked one thing above all: can her data survive APAGAR, or reach somewhere it shouldn't? It found four ways, all fixed, then approved in round 2.

  • blockingA slow message could re-save her data after APAGAR (the race above). Fixed with a per-user lock. The test fails with the lock removed.
  • blockingWith a delete pending, a voice note saying "não, não quero apagar não" contains APAGAR, and deleted everything. Now "apagar" must lead the sentence and nothing may negate it.
  • blockingSwitching from the dev key to a real MASTER_KEY made every existing user crash on load, before APAGAR could run, and the sweep skipped them forever. Fixed with store.Unreadable.
  • blockingAfter MUDAR, every message was read as a mode choice, APAGAR included, and "ler" matched inside "mulher". Keywords and long messages now pass through, with whole-word matching.
  • pushed backConfirm SALVAR when it comes from Claude's intent? No: it's non-destructive, the reply says so, and APAGAR undoes it. The co-worker accepted.
  • disclosedA first-contact voice note reaches ElevenLabs before consent. That's needed to understand a spoken answer, and the PR now says so.

Smaller fixes: the consent-refusal voice note no longer recreates her key, old plaintext photos are encrypted on first read, voice-reply files expire, the Twilio cleanup queue is locked and survives a crash, and idle locks are pruned.

Limits

  • She gets no warning before auto-delete. We don't store her number, so we can't write first. Fixing it means storing the number encrypted and a Meta-approved template.
  • Run one server process. The per-user lock is in memory. Two uvicorn workers would bring the APAGAR race back. It's on the go-live checklist.
  • Without MASTER_KEY, the encryption is decorative. The dev fallback key sits in data/, next to what it protects. Also on the checklist.
  • Twilio deletion is untested against Twilio. Only a fake client exercised it. The WhatsApp checklist covers it.
  • The uvicorn access log shows signed links. They expire in 15 minutes, but they name her user id.
  • Free-plan voice clips are public. public/voice/ carries ElevenLabs free-plan audio: fine for a non-commercial demo with attribution, to be regenerated on a paid plan before any commercial use.
